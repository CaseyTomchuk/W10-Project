This analysis tracks business performance based on sales, profit margin, and return on investment. In most cases, differences observed in sales between 2023 and 2024 can be explained by random variation, but this is not true for ROI.

2024's mean sales were $632,917, and 2023s was $580,000, meaning there was about a $50,000 increase. The p value is insignificant, however. This means that the increase in sales was not statistically significant, it could be explained by random variation. If that is the case, and there was a increase in marketing spending, we could reasonably say that the marketing campaign was ineffective at greatly impacting our sales. We would still say that the company performed well, as our sales were incredibly close (although slightly under) our benchmark.

We also saw that high performing products have a better ROI than low performing products. There are a few ways we could move forward based on this information. It might mean that we should focus our marketing on our top performing products as they are also more cost effective. It is also worth considering why lower performers also have a worse ROI. This is because of economies of scale. Products that have higher demand have more resources put into their production, and there is less room for oversight. Lower performers likely don't have enough resources put into them which leaves room for potential oversight. It is worth doing further analysis to decide if we should invest more in high performing products, or lift up and improve the categories that are falling behind.
